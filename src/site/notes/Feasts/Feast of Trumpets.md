# Feast of Trumpets — Yom Teruah

> Part of the seventh-month feasts — see [[00 — The Feasts Overview\|00 — The Feasts Overview]].

## The governing texts

**Leviticus 23:23–25 (KJV)**
> ²³ And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,
> ²⁴ Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, In the seventh month, in the first day of the month, shall ye have a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation.
> ²⁵ Ye shall do no servile work therein: but ye shall offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD.

**Numbers 29:1–6 (KJV)**
> ¹ And in the seventh month, on the first day of the month, ye shall have an holy convocation; ye shall do no servile work: it is a day of blowing the trumpets unto you.
> ² And ye shall offer a burnt offering for a sweet savour unto the LORD; one young bullock, one ram, and seven lambs of the first year without blemish:
> ³ And their meat offering shall be of flour mingled with oil, three tenth deals for a bullock, and two tenth deals for a ram,
> ⁴ And one tenth deal for one lamb, throughout the seven lambs:
> ⁵ And one kid of the goats for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you:
> ⁶ Beside the burnt offering of the month... a sacrifice made by fire unto the LORD.

## The requirements of the day

The Law lays these on Tishri 1 (Leviticus 23:23–25; Numbers 29:1–6):

1. **A holy convocation** — a consecrated, summoned assembly (*miqra qodesh*; see the terminology in [[00 — The Feasts Overview\|00 — The Feasts Overview]]) — Leviticus 23:24; Numbers 29:1.
2. **A sabbath — rest** — *"shall ye have a sabbath"* (Leviticus 23:24).
3. **No servile work** — Leviticus 23:25; Numbers 29:1.
4. **The blowing of trumpets** — *"a memorial of blowing of trumpets"* (Leviticus 23:24); *"a day of blowing the trumpets"* (Numbers 29:1).
5. **An offering made by fire** (Leviticus 23:25; Numbers 29:2–5):
   - **Burnt offering** — one young bullock, one ram, seven lambs of the first year without blemish (Numbers 29:2)
   - **Meat (meal) offering** — flour mingled with oil: three tenth deals for the bullock, two for the ram, one per lamb (Numbers 29:3–4)
   - **Sin offering** — one kid of the goats, *"to make an atonement for you"* (Numbers 29:5)
6. **Added on top of the standing offerings** — this day's sacrifice is *beside* the new-moon (monthly) offering and the daily continual offering, with their drink offerings (Numbers 29:6; cf. Numbers 28:11–15; 28:3–8). Three layers rise together on this day: the **daily**, the **monthly** new moon, and the **feast**.

> [!warning] No fast on Trumpets
> There is no command to fast or afflict the soul on Trumpets — that belongs to the [[Feasts/Day of Atonement\|Day of Atonement]] ten days later (Leviticus 23:27, 29). Trumpets is rest, assembly, the blast, and offering.

## What we do on the day — and what it means for us

> [!info] For us, a blessing — not a law
> This is how Israel kept the day, and how the Jewish people keep it still. Even for Israel these were never law for their own sake — they were a blessing, His mercy toward His people. As Christians we do **not** keep it as law — we are not bound to make sacrifices, burnt offerings, or the ordinances. But there is great blessing in digging in, because through it we come to understand the Lord so much more. If you are seeking after the heart of the Lord, you will want to see what it *means* to do these things. (The same holds for all the feasts — see [[00 — The Feasts Overview\|00 — The Feasts Overview]].)

Taking the [[#The requirements of the day|requirements]] one by one — not to perform them, but to ask what each means for us:

**A holy convocation — set a day apart for the Lord.**
*Miqra qodesh* is to consecrate, to separate. For us, it is to mark one day out from all the rest and give it wholly to Him.

**The rest — rest *in* the Lord.**
The sabbath-rest was a gift of love. Israel had come out of Egypt, where they served without rest, so the Lord had to *show* them they did not have to work — on this day He let them do no work at all, a complete rest, teaching a once-enslaved people that laying the labour down is His gift, not their loss. What is that rest for us? It is laying down the things we carry — the worries we keep turning over, the striving to be someone we are not — and coming to Him **as we are**. Perhaps we have been selfish all our days; here is a day we lay that down. It is meant to bleed into all of life, but the holy day is marked as a reminder for us to actually do it.

**Shadow:** the sabbath-rest given to a people once worked without rest.
**Fulfilment:** the rest that remains for the people of God — *"There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God"* (Hebrews 4:9).

And this rest is not for the one day alone — it is meant to become the ground of our daily life. This is why the Lord keeps calling it **His** rest — *"my rest"* (Psalm 95:11) — the rest His people are still to enter: *"Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest"* (Hebrews 4:11; and the rest of Jacob — see [[Runner, Messenger, Watchman and the Rest of Jacob\|Runner, Messenger, Watchman and the Rest of Jacob]]). And as you come to dwell within that rest, you shall have *"the peace of God, which passeth all understanding"* (Philippians 4:7) — a peace not for a day but continual, kept daily, *"in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee"* (Isaiah 26:3).

**The blowing of the trumpet — sound the call, together.**
The blast announces; and now we can sound it together, as a people.

> [!quote] In my own practice
> I bought a shofar for this occasion — I want to observe it in community, with the people I love and whom the Lord loves: to remember, to learn more of this day, to prepare our hearts, and to hear what the Lord is speaking to us.

**The offering made by fire — bring yourself.**
Israel brought the burnt offering — seven lambs, one ram, one young bullock, of the first year, without blemish — a sweet savour unto the LORD, with the meal offering and a sin offering. We bring no animals now: Christ is our offering, once for all (Hebrews 10:10, 14). But the pattern still teaches us to present ourselves — *"present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God"* (Romans 12:1).

> [!question] Is the sin offering on this same day?
> Yes. On Trumpets itself a sin offering is commanded — *"one kid of the goats for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you"* (Numbers 29:5) — together with the burnt and meal offerings, the new-moon offering, and the daily. The **great** Day of Atonement, with its two goats (Leviticus 16), comes ten days later. So there is a feast-day atonement on Trumpets, and the deep atonement on [[Feasts/Day of Atonement\|Day of Atonement]].

**How we keep it — the gathering of the saints.**
We keep it by coming together; it becomes an occasion for the gathering of the saints — to remember, to learn, to prepare our hearts, and to seek what the Lord is speaking.

> [!quote] A conviction held
> I truly believe that if the Lord were to return, it would be in this season. We do not know the year — but it would be in this time.

## The seventh month in our times (September–October)

The seventh month is **Tishri** on the Hebrew calendar, which falls across **September–October** on ours (this year, Tishri 1 begins at sundown 11 September 2026). It is reckoned the *seventh* month because the sacred year is counted from Nisan in the spring (Exodus 12:2) — so this month sits in the autumn, at the turn of the agricultural year, when the harvest is gathered in. The autumn feasts therefore carry the sense of ingathering, and of an appointed end toward which the whole year has been moving.

## The New Moon

Trumpets is set at the **beginning** of the month — Tishri 1, the **new moon**, when the light is only a first sliver returning after the dark. That is the sign of it: the *start of a new thing*, a new light entering — the coming of the King.

Most of the other feasts fall the other way, at **mid-month, under the full moon** — Unleavened Bread on Nisan 15, Tabernacles on Tishri 15 — kept in the fullness of the light, the light of the day and the light of the night together.

So the timing itself preaches. At Trumpets the Lord comes as **light into a place of darkness**, the new light breaking in; the feasts that follow are kept *under* that light, once it has already come.

## Not a standalone feast — the feasts tell one story

Trumpets opens the seventh-month feasts, and they move as one narrative:

1. **Feast of Trumpets** (Leviticus 23:23–25) — the *announcement of the King*.
2. **[[Feasts/Day of Atonement\|Day of Atonement]]** (Leviticus 23:26–32) — the *day of judgment*, and atonement for those who receive it.
3. **[[Feasts/Feast of Tabernacles\|Feast of Tabernacles]]** (Leviticus 23:33–43) — the *gathering to the Father's house*, to dwell with Him.

The trumpet sounds first; it summons the assembly toward Atonement ten days later, and Atonement opens into the dwelling of Tabernacles. This note begins with the trumpet.

## Before the trumpet — preparing the way

The trumpet of the seventh month does not sound into an unready world. Before the King is announced, the road is made ready. The month before Tishri — **Elul** (roughly August–September) — opens a forty-day season of *teshuvah* (repentance, "returning"), in which by tradition the shofar is blown each morning to rouse the people to turn; the season runs through Trumpets and closes at Atonement.

This is the pattern Isaiah gives: **Isaiah 40:3–4** — *"Prepare ye the way of the LORD... Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low."* The road for the King is levelled — the mountain brought low, the valley raised up, the crooked made straight — so that when He comes the way is clear. For the coming of the King to come, the way must first be prepared. These are the things that must happen *before* the trumpet sounds.

## The two witnesses and the hidden hour — the day no man knows

Because Trumpets opens on the **new moon**, its onset could not be fixed by calculation. By tradition (Mishnah, Rosh Hashanah), the new month was declared only after **two witnesses** came before the court and testified that they had seen the first sliver. They kept their watch **in the night**, and no one could name the hour it would appear — cloud might hide it, and the moon comes when it comes. Until the testimony was received and confirmed, the day could not be announced; so its beginning was never known in advance.

This is the picture behind the Lord's own words:

- **Deuteronomy 19:15** — *"at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established."*
- **Matthew 24:36** — *"But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only."* (cf. **Mark 13:32**)
- **Matthew 24:42–44** — *"Watch therefore... therefore be ye also ready."*
- **1 Thessalonians 5:2** — *"the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night."*
- **Revelation 11:3** — *"And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy..."*

But "as a thief in the night" does not mean the **season** is unknown — it means the **hour** is hidden. The children of light are not caught out, because they read the season:

- **1 Thessalonians 5:1–5** — *"...ye, brethren, are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you as a thief. Ye are all the children of light..."*
- The fig tree — **Matthew 24:32–33** — *"when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors."*
- The signs of the season — **Matthew 24:4–5, 11–12, 24** — *"many false prophets shall rise, and shall deceive many... iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold... false Christs, and false prophets."*

So we know the season — deception, false prophets, love grown cold — and we are told to watch. In that season we have a work: to **usher in** the coming of the King, for the word of the Lord has come. Part of that ushering is to prepare the way — see [[Feasts/Prepare the Way\|Prepare the Way]].

**Shadow:** the new moon of the seventh month, unknowable until two witnesses testify, then proclaimed with the trumpet blast.
**Fulfilment:** the King attested by two witnesses — *"the testimony of two men is true... I bear witness of myself, and the Father that sent me beareth witness of me"* (John 8:17–18) — coming on a day no man knows, in a season the watchful can read.

## The uses of the trumpet — one instrument, many calls

The trumpet is put to several distinct uses across Scripture — the war alarm, the watchman's warning, the coronation of a king, the announcing of the LORD's coming, the calling of the assembly, the proclaiming of liberty at Jubilee, and the raising of the dead at the last trump. When the *Feast* of Trumpets sounds, all of these gather into one blast.

**See the full list, with references: [[Topic/Uses of the Trumpet\|Uses of the Trumpet]].**

But for this feast, one use stands at the centre and governs the rest: the trumpet is the **announcement of the King**.

> [!abstract]- The trumpet throughout Scripture — the King, the ascent, the gathering, the joyful noise
> The trumpet never sounds emptily; wherever it is blown, the same notes gather around it — the King announced, God gone up, the people gathered, a joyful noise raised, the day of the Lord at hand.
>
> **The pattern is given — the two silver trumpets**
> - **Numbers 10:2, 8, 10** — *"Make thee two trumpets of silver... And the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall blow with the trumpets... in the day of your gladness, and in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months... that they may be to you for a memorial before your God."*
>
> **Blow the trumpet in the new moon**
> - **Psalm 81:3–4** — *"Blow up the trumpet in the new moon, in the time appointed, on our solemn feast day. For this was a statute for Israel, and a law of the God of Jacob."*
>
> **The announcement of the King**
> - **1 Kings 1:34, 39** — *"blow ye with the trumpet, and say, God save king Solomon."*
> - **2 Kings 9:13** — *"they blew with trumpets, saying, Jehu is king."*
>
> **God is gone up — the ascent with the trumpet**
> - **Psalm 47:5** — *"God is gone up with a shout, the LORD with the sound of a trumpet."*
> - **2 Samuel 6:15** — *"David and all the house of Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with shouting, and with the sound of the trumpet."*
> - **Psalm 24:7–10** — *"Lift up your heads, O ye gates... and the King of glory shall come in."*
> - **Psalm 68:18** — *"Thou hast ascended on high, thou hast led captivity captive"* (cf. **Ephesians 4:8**).
>
> **Everybody making a joyful noise**
> - **Psalm 47:1** — *"O clap your hands, all ye people; shout unto God with the voice of triumph."*
> - **Psalm 98:4–6** — *"Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all the earth... with trumpets and sound of cornet make a joyful noise before the LORD, the King."*
> - **Psalm 100:1** — *"Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands."*
> - **Psalm 150:3** — *"Praise him with the sound of the trumpet."*
>
> **The trumpet of the day of the LORD — God is coming**
> - **Joel 2:1** — *"Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm in my holy mountain... for the day of the LORD cometh."*
> - **Joel 2:15** — *"Blow the trumpet in Zion, sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly."*
> - **Zephaniah 1:14, 16** — *"The great day of the LORD is near... a day of the trumpet and alarm."*
> - **Zechariah 9:14** — *"the Lord GOD shall blow the trumpet, and shall go with whirlwinds of the south."*
> - **Exodus 19:16, 19** — Sinai: *"the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud... and when the voice of the trumpet sounded long."*
>
> **The great trumpet — the gathering of the people**
> - **Isaiah 27:13** — *"the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish... and shall worship the LORD in the holy mount at Jerusalem."*
> - **Matthew 24:31** — *"he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds."*
> - **1 Corinthians 15:52** — *"at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible."*
> - **1 Thessalonians 4:16** — *"the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first."*
>
> **The seventh trumpet — the kingdom announced**
> - **Revelation 11:15** — *"The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever."*
