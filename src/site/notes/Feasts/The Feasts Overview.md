# The Feasts — Overview

> [!info] For us, a blessing — not a law
> These are the feasts God gave Israel, and the Jewish people keep them still. Even for Israel these were never law for their own sake — they were a blessing, His mercy toward His people. As Christians we do **not** observe them as law — we are not bound to sacrifices, burnt offerings, or the ordinances. But there is real blessing in digging in: through these appointed times we come to understand the Lord so much more. If you are seeking the heart of the Lord, you will want to see what it means to keep them.

## The appointed times

Leviticus 23 gathers the LORD's appointed times into two seasons:

- **Spring** — Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Pentecost (Weeks)
- **Autumn (the seventh month)** — Trumpets, Atonement, Tabernacles (crowned by the Eighth Day)

These notes take up the **autumn feasts** — the feasts of the seventh month — which do not stand alone but move as one narrative.

## The words: *mo'ed* and *miqra qodesh*

Two Hebrew words govern how Scripture speaks of these days:

- **מוֹעֵד — *mo'ed*** (plural *mo'adim*) — an **appointed time**, a set meeting fixed by God, not chosen by man. Leviticus 23:2 calls the whole list *"the feasts of the LORD"* — in Hebrew *mo'adei YHWH*, **the appointed times of the LORD**. The root (*ya'ad*, to appoint/meet) is the same one behind the *ohel mo'ed*, the **tent of meeting** — a *mo'ed* is an appointed time to *meet with God*. Its first use is Genesis 1:14 — the sun and moon are set *"for signs, and for seasons"* (*mo'adim*) — so the appointed times are written into the lights of heaven, which is why the new moon governs Trumpets.
- **מִקְרָא קֹדֶשׁ — *miqra qodesh*** — the phrase rendered **"holy convocation."** *Qodesh* is **holy, set apart, consecrated**; *miqra* comes from *qara*, **to call / to proclaim** — so it is a **called, summoned, consecrated assembly**.

Note the fitness for Trumpets: the day is a *miqra* — a **calling** — and the instrument that does the calling is the **trumpet**. The consecrated day and the summoning blast are one idea.

A precision to keep: **"the appointed times of the LORD" (*mo'adim*)** is a different phrase from **"the day of the LORD" (*yom YHWH*)**, the prophets' day of judgment (Joel 2:1; Zephaniah 1). They meet richly at Trumpets, but they are not the same word.

## The feasts tell one story

The trumpet sounds first and summons the assembly; ten days later comes Atonement; five days after that, Tabernacles opens into dwelling, and the seven days are crowned by the Eighth Day. Read together they trace a single movement:

1. **[[Feasts/Feast of Trumpets\|Feast of Trumpets]]** — the *announcement of the King* (Leviticus 23:23–25)
2. **[[Feasts/Day of Atonement\|Day of Atonement]]** — the *day of judgment*, and atonement for those who receive it (Leviticus 23:26–32)
3. **[[Feasts/Feast of Tabernacles\|Feast of Tabernacles]]** — the *gathering to the Father's house*, to dwell with Him (Leviticus 23:33–43), opening into the **Eighth Day** — a forever beyond the seven (Leviticus 23:36; John 7:37)

## The pattern

| Feast | Tishri | The beat of the story |
|---|---|---|
| Trumpets | 1 | The King is announced — the trumpet is sounded on a day no man knows |
| Atonement | 10 | The day of judgment — and atonement covering those who receive it |
| Tabernacles | 15–21 | The gathering home — dwelling with Him in the Father's house |
| The Eighth Day | 22 | One day more — the dwelling opening into a forever beyond the week |

Each feast gets its own deep-dive; this note holds the shape of the whole.
